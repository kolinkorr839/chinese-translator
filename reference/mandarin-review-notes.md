# Mandarin Workbook - Review Notes for Native Speaker

Items where I'm less confident in the Mandarin and would appreciate a native speaker spot-check:

## Tone Assignments
- 上 in 早上好 - marked as neutral tone (shang5). Some sources mark it as shang4 in isolation but neutral in this compound. Verify.
- 面 in 前面/后面 - marked as neutral. Some dialects keep 4th tone. Verify for standard putonghua.

## Phrase Accuracy
1. "请在这里停" (please stop here) - Verify this is natural for telling a taxi driver to stop. Some dialects prefer 停一下 or 就在这儿.
2. "买单" vs "结账" - I used 买单 throughout. 结账 (jiézhàng) is also common. 买单 is more colloquial/southern. Verify for Beijing context.
3. "要点什么" - Natural for a server asking what you'd like? Some sources use 你们要吃什么.
4. "我要了" (I'll take it) in the shopping dialogue - Verify this is natural for agreeing to buy.
5. "可以用微信支付吗" - Verify this is the natural way to ask about WeChat Pay (vs 微信可以吗).

## Traditional Characters
6. 計程車 is the Taiwan term for taxi (vs 出租車 for mainland). I used 出租車 throughout. If the reader goes to Taiwan, they should know 計程車.
7. Some traditional/simplified pairs are identical (like 你好, 茶, 水). These are correct - the characters genuinely look the same in both systems.

## Grammar Explanations
8. The "adjectives as verbs" explanation (Lesson 11) simplifies a complex topic. The 很 (hen) explanation is accurate for basic usage but there are nuances with comparative usage.
9. The 了 (le) particle explanation covers completed action / change of state but omits the ongoing aspect (在...呢). This is appropriate for the beginner level.

## Cultural Notes
10. "Bill-fighting ritual" - Verify this is still common with younger generations in major cities, or if it's more of an older/traditional custom.
11. WeChat Pay / Alipay for foreigners - Rules for linking foreign cards change frequently. The workbook advises setting it up before the trip, but the specific process should be verified against current requirements.
12. "你吃了吗" as a greeting - Some younger Chinese people in major cities may find this old-fashioned. Still widely used in northern China and by older generations.

## Pronunciation Approximations
13. The English approximations are inherently imprecise. The most problematic ones:
    - "shir" for 是 (shì) - the -i after sh is hard to approximate in English
    - "juh guh" for 这个 (zhège) - the zh initial and neutral tone ge are tricky
    - "dzuh" for 子 (zi) - the z initial is often harder than English "dz"
    These are best-effort and work for getting started, but learners should listen to native audio.
