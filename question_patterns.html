Question Patterns

How Chinese organizes questions - five patterns, one mental model

Quick Reference

Function English Pattern Chinese Pattern Example
Yes/no question Do / Does...? Subject + verb + 吗？ 你喜欢音乐吗？
Specific information What / Where / Who...? Subject + verb + question word 你住在哪里？
Condition / quality How is...? What is...like? Topic + 怎么样 天气怎么样？
Identity Are / Is + noun? Subject + 是 + noun + 吗？ 你是美国人吗？
Description / state Are / Is + adjective? Subject + adjective + 吗？ 你忙吗？
Ability / possibility Can you...? Subject + 能/会 + verb + 吗？ 你会说英语吗？
Completed event Have you...? Subject + verb + 了 + 吗？ 你吃饭了吗？
A-not-A (yes/no) Do you...or not? Subject + A + 不 + A？ 你是不是喜欢咖啡？

How to Convert an English Question

  1. Identify the question type. Is it asking yes/no, what, where, how, ability, identity, or a past event?
  2. Reorder into Chinese-style English. Put the subject first, then the verb, and move the question word to where the answer belongs.
  3. Apply the Chinese pattern. Add 吗 for yes/no, or use a question word such as 什么 (what), 哪里 (where), or 怎么样 (how).
English
"What are you doing?"
Chinese-style English
"You are doing what?"
Chinese
你在做什么？
Don't translate auxiliaries mechanically. In this sentence, 在 marks an ongoing action in Chinese - it is not a word-for-word translation of English "are."

Six Patterns

  1. Do / Does questions - add 吗
  2. WH-questions - question word in answer position
  3. How / what is it like? - 怎么样
  4. Are / is questions - identity vs. description
  5. Can / have / past-action questions
  6. A-not-A questions - A + 不 + A

Pattern 1: Do / Does Questions - Add 吗

Rule: Keep the subject and verb in their normal Chinese order, then add 吗 at the end. Chinese doesn't need helper words like do or does.
Shortcut: Remove do/does from the English, put 吗 at the end.
Do you like it here?
你喜欢这里吗？
Nǐ xǐhuan zhèlǐ ma?
你 nǐ you
喜欢 xǐhuan like
这里 zhèlǐ here
吗？ ma question
Mental conversion: You like here?
EnglishChinesePinyin
Do you speak English? 你说英语吗？ Nǐ shuō Yīngyǔ ma?
Do you like coffee? 你喜欢咖啡吗？ Nǐ xǐhuan kāfēi ma?
Does he work here? 他在这里工作吗？ Tā zài zhèlǐ gōngzuò ma?
Do you have pets? 你有宠物吗？ Nǐ yǒu chǒngwù ma?
Answering 吗 questions: Confirm or negate the main verb instead of translating English yes or no.

你喜欢咖啡吗？ - Do you like coffee?
喜欢。 - Like it. (Yes)
不喜欢。 - Don't like it. (No)

Pattern 2: WH-Questions - Question Word in Answer Position

Rule: Chinese keeps normal sentence order and puts the question word where the answer would go. This is one of the biggest differences from English.
Shortcut: Subject first, verb in place, question word where the answer goes.
Where do you live?
你住在哪里？
Nǐ zhù zài nǎlǐ?
English
Where → do you → live?
Question word first
Chinese
你 → 住在 → 哪里？
Question word where the answer goes
Mental conversion: You live where?

If the answer is "Beijing," the question word 哪里 occupies that same position:

你住在哪里？ - Where do you live?
我住在北京。 - I live in Beijing.
EnglishChineseMental Conversion
What are you doing? 你在做什么？ You are doing what?
Where are you going? 你去哪里？ You go where?
Who is that? 那是谁？ That is who?
What do you want? 你想要什么？ You want what?
When are you leaving? 你什么时候走？ You when leave?

Question Words

什么 shénme what
哪里 nǎlǐ where
谁 shéi who
什么时候 shénme shíhou when
为什么 wèishénme why
怎么 zěnme how

Where Does 在哪里 Go?

The position of 在哪里 depends on how the location relates to the verb. Think of 在 as "at" or "in."

Question TypePatternExample
Where someone lives/is Subject + verb + 在哪里 你住在哪里？
Where an action happens Subject + 在哪里 + verb + object 你在哪里学习中文？
English
"Where do you study Chinese?"
Remove "do", reorder
"You where study Chinese?"
Chinese
你在哪里学习中文？
Question words don't always go at the end. They occupy the position appropriate to the information being asked about. Here, 在哪里 comes before the action 学习中文 because it describes where the action happens.
Don't add 吗 to WH-questions. The question word already signals a question. 你住在哪里？ is correct. 你住在哪里吗？ is not the standard form.

Pattern 3: How / What Is It Like? - 怎么样

Rule: Use 怎么样 (zěnmeyàng) when asking about a condition, experience, quality, or general state.
Shortcut: "How is X?" or "What is X like?" → X + 怎么样
How's it going?
最近怎么样？
Zuìjìn zěnmeyàng?
最近 zuìjìn recently
怎么样 zěnmeyàng how / how is it
Mental conversion: Recently, how?
EnglishChinesePinyin
How was your day? 你今天过得怎么样？ Nǐ jīntiān guò de zěnmeyàng?
What's the weather like? 天气怎么样？ Tiānqì zěnmeyàng?
How is the food? 食物怎么样？ Shíwù zěnmeyàng?
How is your Chinese? 你的中文怎么样？ Nǐ de Zhōngwén zěnmeyàng?

怎么样 vs. 怎么

怎么样 - state / quality
这个怎么样？
How is this? / What do you think?
怎么 - method / manner
这个怎么做？
How do you make this?

Pattern 4: Are / Is Questions - Identity vs. Description

Rule: English uses am/is/are constantly, but Chinese handles identity and description differently. Use 是 for identity (with a noun). Omit 是 for descriptions (with an adjective).
Shortcut: Identifies what someone is → 是. Describes how someone is → no 是.

A. Identity: use 是

Use 是 (shì) when identifying someone or something, paired with a noun.

Are you American?
你是美国人吗？
Nǐ shì Měiguó rén ma?
你 nǐ you
是 shì are / be
美国人 Měiguórén American
吗？ ma question

Structure: Subject + 是 + noun + 吗？

你是学生吗？ - Are you a student?
他是老师吗？ - Is he a teacher?
这是你的手机吗？ - Is this your phone?

B. Description: usually omit 是

When describing with an adjective, place it directly after the subject. No 是 needed.

Are you busy?
你忙吗？
Nǐ máng ma?
你 nǐ you
忙 máng busy
吗？ ma question

Structure: Subject + adjective + 吗？

你累吗？ - Are you tired?
你高兴吗？ - Are you happy?
你冷吗？ - Are you cold?
你好吗？ - How are you? / Are you well?

Why This Matters

EnglishChineseWhy
Are you American? 你是美国人吗？ Identity / noun
Are you busy? 你忙吗？ Adjective
Are you a teacher? 你是老师吗？ Identity / noun
Are you happy? 你高兴吗？ Adjective

Pattern 5: Can / Have / Past-Action Questions

Rule: Chinese expresses ability, possession, and completed actions with its own words and markers instead of English auxiliary verbs. Identify what the English helper word actually means.
Shortcut: What does have mean? Possession → 有. Learned ability → 会. Experience → 过. Completed event → 了.

A. Can you...? - 能 or 会

Can you help me?
你能帮我吗？
Nǐ néng bāng wǒ ma?
你 nǐ you
能 néng can / able
帮我 bāng wǒ help me
吗？ ma question
能 (néng) - circumstances / ability
你能来吗？
Can you come? (Are you able to?)
会 (huì) - learned skill
你会说英语吗？
Can you speak English? (Do you know how?)
你会开车吗？ - Can you drive? (learned skill)

B. Have you...? - Possession vs. Experience

For possession, use 有 (yǒu):

你有车吗？ - Do you have a car?
你有兄弟姐妹吗？ - Do you have siblings?

C. Have you eaten? - 了 (le) for completed events

Have you eaten?
你吃饭了吗？
Nǐ chīfàn le ma?
你 nǐ you
吃饭 chīfàn eat / meal
了 le completed
吗？ ma question

Structure: Subject + verb + 了 + 吗？

你买了吗？ - Have you bought it?
你到了吗？ - Have you arrived?
了 vs. 过: Not every "have you..." needs 了. For past experiences, use 过 (guo).

"Have you ever been to China?" → 你去过中国吗？

过 marks experience ("ever done X"). 了 marks a change or completed event. They are not interchangeable.

Pattern 6: A-not-A Questions - A + 不 + A

Rule: Instead of adding 吗, repeat the key word in positive-negative form: A + 不 + A. The "A" can be a verb, adjective, or modal. This offers a choice between "do" and "don't" directly inside the sentence.
Shortcut: Take the key word, sandwich 不 in the middle: 是不是, 喜不喜欢, 好不好, 能不能.
Do you like coffee (or not)?
你是不是喜欢咖啡？
Nǐ shì bu shì xǐhuan kāfēi?
你 nǐ you
是不是 shì bu shì is or isn't
喜欢 xǐhuan like
咖啡 kāfēi coffee
Mental conversion: You is-or-isn't like coffee?
EnglishChinesePinyin
Do you like it or not? 你喜不喜欢？ Nǐ xǐ bu xǐhuan?
Is this good or not? 这个好不好？ Zhège hǎo bu hǎo?
Are you busy or not? 你忙不忙？ Nǐ máng bu máng?
Can you see it or not? 你看得见看不见？ Nǐ kàn de jiàn kàn bu jiàn?
Is he a student? 他是不是学生？ Tā shì bu shì xuéshēng?
吗 vs. A-not-A: Both ask yes/no questions. 吗 is neutral and more common in casual speech. A-not-A is more direct - it presents both options explicitly, like asking "do you or don't you?" It sounds slightly more pressing or deliberate.

你喜欢咖啡吗？ - Do you like coffee? (neutral)
你喜不喜欢咖啡？ - Do you like coffee or not? (more direct)

For multi-syllable words, only the first syllable repeats: 喜欢 becomes 喜不喜欢, not 喜欢不喜欢.
