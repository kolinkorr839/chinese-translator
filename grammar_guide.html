Pattern 1

Basic Word Order (S + T + V + O)

Formula Subject + Time + Verb + Object

Chinese sentences follow a strict order. Time always comes before the verb, not after like English. You say "I yesterday ate" rather than "I ate yesterday."

Examples
  • 1
    我昨天吃了饺子|我昨天吃了餃子
    wǒ zuótiān chī le jiǎozi
    "I ate dumplings yesterday"
  • 2
    她每天喝茶|她每天喝茶
    tā měitiān hē chá
    "She drinks tea every day"
  • 3
    我们明天去机场|我們明天去機場
    wǒmen míngtiān qù jīchǎng
    "We're going to the airport tomorrow"
  • 4
    他昨天晚上看了电影|他昨天晚上看了電影
    tā zuótiān wǎnshang kàn le diànyǐng
    "He watched a movie last night"
  • 5
    你今天想吃什么？|你今天想吃什麼？
    nǐ jīntiān xiǎng chī shénme?
    "What do you want to eat today?"
Tip

If you remember one rule, remember this: time goes before the verb. "I yesterday ate" not "I ate yesterday."

Pattern 2

是...的 (Emphasis on Circumstances)

Formula Subject + 是 + Detail + 的

Used to emphasize when, where, how, or who did something (not what happened). The action is already known; you're highlighting the circumstances.

Examples
  • 1
    我是昨天来的|我是昨天來的
    wǒ shì zuótiān lái de
    "I came YESTERDAY" (emphasizing when)
  • 2
    她是在台北学中文的|她是在台北學中文的
    tā shì zài Táiběi xué zhōngwén de
    "She learned Chinese IN TAIPEI" (where)
  • 3
    你是怎么来的？|你是怎麼來的？
    nǐ shì zěnme lái de?
    "HOW did you get here?" (how)
  • 4
    这本书是谁买的？|這本書是誰買的？
    zhè běn shū shì shéi mǎi de?
    "WHO bought this book?" (who)
  • 5
    我是坐高铁来的|我是坐高鐵來的
    wǒ shì zuò gāotiě lái de
    "I came BY high-speed train" (how)
Tip

Think of 是...的 as a spotlight - the action already happened, you're just shining a light on one detail.

Pattern 3

在 + Verb (Ongoing Action)

Formula Subject + 在 + Verb

Like English "-ing." Describes what's happening right now.

Examples
  • 1
    我在吃饭|我在吃飯
    wǒ zài chīfàn
    "I'm eating"
  • 2
    她在睡觉|她在睡覺
    tā zài shuìjiào
    "She's sleeping"
  • 3
    你在做什么？|你在做什麼？
    nǐ zài zuò shénme?
    "What are you doing?"
  • 4
    他们在看电影|他們在看電影
    tāmen zài kàn diànyǐng
    "They're watching a movie"
  • 5
    外面在下雨|外面在下雨
    wàimiàn zài xiàyǔ
    "It's raining outside"
Tip

Don't confuse 在 (doing now) with 了 (did it). 我在吃 = "I'm eating." 我吃了 = "I ate."

Quick reference - how Chinese marks verb timing:

Meaning Marker Example
basic action - 我吃饭 | 我吃飯 - I eat
ongoing 在 (Pattern 3) 我在吃饭 | 我在吃飯 - I am eating
completed 了 (Pattern 4) 我吃了饭 | 我吃了飯 - I ate
experienced before 过 (Pattern 5) 我吃过北京烤鸭 | 我吃過北京烤鴨 - I have eaten Peking duck
about to / want to 要 (Pattern 6) 我要吃饭 | 我要吃飯 - I am going to eat
ability 会 / 能 (Pattern 6) 我会吃辣 | 我會吃辣 - I can eat spicy food

Pattern 4

了 (Completed Action & Change of State)

Formula A Verb + 了
Formula B Adjective / Situation + 了

了 has two different uses. After a verb = "did it, done." At the end of a sentence = "things have changed." Many learners confuse these.

Completed Action (Verb + 了)
  • 1
    我吃了饭|我吃了飯
    wǒ chī le fàn
    "I ate" (action completed)
  • 2
    他买了三本书|他買了三本書
    tā mǎi le sān běn shū
    "He bought three books"
  • 3
    我已经到了|我已經到了
    wǒ yǐjīng dào le
    "I've already arrived"
Change of State (Sentence + 了)
  • 4
    天气冷了|天氣冷了
    tiānqì lěng le
    "It's gotten cold" (it wasn't cold before)
  • 5
    我不想吃了|我不想吃了
    wǒ bù xiǎng chī le
    "I don't want to eat anymore" (I did before, now I don't)
Tip

Verb + 了 = "did it." Sentence + 了 = "now it's different." When both appear (我吃了饭了), it means "I've eaten now" - completed AND new situation.

Pattern 5

过 (Past Experience)

Formula Verb + 过

"Have you ever...?" Describes whether you've had the experience before, not when it happened.

Examples
  • 1
    你去过中国吗？|你去過中國嗎？
    nǐ qù guo zhōngguó ma?
    "Have you ever been to China?"
  • 2
    我吃过饺子|我吃過餃子
    wǒ chī guo jiǎozi
    "I've had dumplings before"
  • 3
    她没有学过中文|她沒有學過中文
    tā méiyǒu xué guo zhōngwén
    "She has never studied Chinese"
  • 4
    你看过这部电影吗？|你看過這部電影嗎？
    nǐ kàn guo zhè bù diànyǐng ma?
    "Have you seen this movie?"
  • 5
    我没坐过高铁|我沒坐過高鐵
    wǒ méi zuò guo gāotiě
    "I've never taken the high-speed train"
Tip

过 vs 了: 我吃了饺子 = "I ate dumplings (just now)." 我吃过饺子 = "I've had dumplings (at some point in my life)."

Pattern 6

想 / 要 / 会 / 能 / 可以 (The 5 "Can/Want" Words)

Formula Subject + [想/要/会/能/可以] + Verb

These five words all translate to "want" or "can" in English but are not interchangeable. Each carries a distinct shade of meaning.

想 (xiǎng)
"want to" / "thinking of" - softer, polite
  • 1
    我想吃面|我想吃麵
    wǒ xiǎng chī miàn
    "I'd like to eat noodles"
  • 2
    我想去台北|我想去台北
    wǒ xiǎng qù Táiběi
    "I'm thinking of going to Taipei"
要 (yào)
"want to" / "going to" - stronger, definite
  • 1
    我要一杯茶|我要一杯茶
    wǒ yào yì bēi chá
    "I want a cup of tea"
  • 2
    我要去了|我要去了
    wǒ yào qù le
    "I'm about to go"
会 / 會 (huì)
"know how to" / "will" - learned skill or future prediction
  • 1
    你会说中文吗？|你會說中文嗎？
    nǐ huì shuō zhōngwén ma?
    "Can you speak Chinese?" (skill)
  • 2
    明天会下雨|明天會下雨
    míngtiān huì xiàyǔ
    "It will rain tomorrow" (prediction)
能 (néng)
"can" - physical ability or circumstances allow
  • 1
    你能帮我吗？|你能幫我嗎？
    nǐ néng bāng wǒ ma?
    "Can you help me?" (are you able to)
  • 2
    我今天不能去|我今天不能去
    wǒ jīntiān bù néng qù
    "I can't go today" (circumstances)
可以 (kěyǐ)
"may" / "allowed to" - permission
  • 1
    我可以坐这里吗？|我可以坐這裡嗎？
    wǒ kěyǐ zuò zhèlǐ ma?
    "May I sit here?"
  • 2
    这里不可以抽烟|這裡不可以抽煙
    zhèlǐ bù kěyǐ chōuyān
    "No smoking here" (not allowed)
Tip

Quick test: "Can you swim?" = 你会游泳吗 (skill). "Can you swim today?" = 你今天能游泳吗 (circumstance). "May I swim here?" = 我可以在这里游泳吗 (permission).

Pattern 7

把 (Active Voice)

Formula Subject + Time + 把 + Object + Verb + Result

把 means "take [this] and [do that to it]." It moves the object before the verb to emphasize what was done to it. English doesn't really have this - you just have to learn to "grab" your objects before you "verb" them. Use it when the action changes, moves, or disposes of the object in some way. See 把 vs 被 comparison below.

Examples
  • 1
    请把门关上|請把門關上
    qǐng bǎ mén guān shàng
    "Please close the door"
  • 2
    他把书放在桌子上|他把書放在桌子上
    tā bǎ shū fàng zài zhuōzi shàng
    "He put the book on the table"
  • 3
    我把功课做完了|我把功課做完了
    wǒ bǎ gōngkè zuò wán le
    "I finished my homework"
  • 4
    她把咖啡喝完了|她把咖啡喝完了
    tā bǎ kāfēi hē wán le
    "She finished drinking the coffee"
  • 5
    你把名字写在这里|你把名字寫在這裡
    nǐ bǎ míngzì xiě zài zhèlǐ
    "Write your name here"
Tip

把 needs a result - you can't just say 把门关. You need 把门关上 (close-up). The verb alone isn't enough; something must happen to the object.

Pattern 8

被 (Passive Voice)

Formula Victim + Time + 被 + Doer + Verb + Result

被 is closest to English "was" - it marks the passive voice. Traditionally used for unfortunate events (things happening to you), though modern usage has expanded. See 把 vs 被 comparison below.

Examples
  • 1
    我的手机被偷了|我的手機被偷了
    wǒ de shǒujī bèi tōu le
    "My phone was stolen"
  • 2
    蛋糕被他吃了|蛋糕被他吃了
    dàngāo bèi tā chī le
    "The cake was eaten by him"
  • 3
    那本书被借走了|那本書被借走了
    nà běn shū bèi jiè zǒu le
    "That book was borrowed (taken away)"
  • 4
    他被老师骂了|他被老師罵了
    tā bèi lǎoshī mà le
    "He was scolded by the teacher"
  • 5
    这首歌被很多人喜欢|這首歌被很多人喜歡
    zhè shǒu gē bèi hěn duō rén xǐhuān
    "This song is liked by many people"
Tip

被 traditionally carries a negative feeling - something bad happened to you. Modern usage is looser, but when in doubt, 被 implies the subject didn't want it to happen.

Patterns 7 & 8

把 vs 被 - Active & Passive Voice

Core difference: 把 (bǎ) is about control - you use it when the subject "handles" or "disposes of" an object. 被 (bèi) is about impact - you use it when the subject is "affected" (usually negatively) by an action.

The Sentence Blueprints
Feature 把 (bǎ) - Active 被 (bèi) - Passive
Logic Doer handles the Object Victim is affected by the Doer
Word Order Doer + Time + 把 + Object + Verb + Result Victim + Time + 被 + Doer + Verb + Result
Example 我昨天把手机摔了。 我的手机昨天被我摔了。
Pinyin Wǒ zuótiān bǎ shǒujī shuāi le. Wǒ de shǒujī zuótiān bèi wǒ shuāi le.
English I dropped the phone yesterday. My phone was dropped by me yesterday.
Quick Mental Model

被 is closest to English "was [verb]ed" - "the cake was eaten." Easy to map.

把 is "Take [this] and [do that to it]" (The Executive) - English doesn't really have this. You just have to learn to "grab" your objects before you "verb" them!

Pattern 9

的 (Possession & Description)

Formula A Noun + 的 + Noun
Formula B Adjective + 的 + Noun

的 (de) is the most common Chinese particle. It links a modifier to a noun - either showing possession (like English "'s") or describing the noun (like "the red car" = 红的车).

Possession
  • 1
    这是我的书|這是我的書
    zhè shì wǒ de shū
    "This is my book"
  • 2
    老师的名字叫什么？|老師的名字叫什麼？
    lǎoshī de míngzì jiào shénme?
    "What is the teacher's name?"
Description
  • 3
    好看的电影|好看的電影
    hǎokàn de diànyǐng
    "a good-looking movie"
  • 4
    穿红色衣服的女生|穿紅色衣服的女生
    chuān hóngsè yīfú de nǚshēng
    "the girl wearing red clothes"
  • 5
    昨天买的那个|昨天買的那個
    zuótiān mǎi de nàge
    "the one (I) bought yesterday"
Tip

For close relationships, 的 is often dropped: 我妈 (my mom), 我家 (my home). But for emphasis or clarity, keep it: 这是我的 ("This is mine!").

Pattern 10

得 (Degree & Result Complement)

Formula Verb + 得 + Description

得 (de) goes after a verb to describe how well or to what degree the action was performed. It answers "how did you do it?"

Examples
  • 1
    你说得很好|你說得很好
    nǐ shuō de hěn hǎo
    "You speak very well"
  • 2
    他跑得很快|他跑得很快
    tā pǎo de hěn kuài
    "He runs very fast"
  • 3
    她唱得不好听|她唱得不好聽
    tā chàng de bù hǎotīng
    "She doesn't sing well"
  • 4
    我吃得太饱了|我吃得太飽了
    wǒ chī de tài bǎo le
    "I ate too much (I'm too full)"
  • 5
    你写得真漂亮|你寫得真漂亮
    nǐ xiě de zhēn piàoliang
    "Your writing is really beautiful"
Tip

If the verb has an object, repeat the verb: 他说中文说得很好 (He speaks Chinese speaks-well). You can't put 得 between the verb and its object.

Pattern 11

地 (Adverb Marker)

Formula Adverb/Adj + 地 + Verb

地 (de) goes before a verb to turn an adjective into an adverb - describing how the action is performed. Think of it as the Chinese "-ly" suffix.

Examples
  • 1
    她慢慢地走|她慢慢地走
    tā mànmàn de zǒu
    "She walks slowly"
  • 2
    他高兴地笑了|他高興地笑了
    tā gāoxìng de xiào le
    "He laughed happily"
  • 3
    请认真地听|請認真地聽
    qǐng rènzhēn de tīng
    "Please listen carefully"
  • 4
    她安静地看书|她安靜地看書
    tā ānjìng de kànshū
    "She reads quietly"
  • 5
    孩子们快乐地玩|孩子們快樂地玩
    háizimen kuàilè de wán
    "The children play happily"
Tip

The three de particles: 的 modifies nouns (beautiful 的 girl), 得 follows verbs (runs 得 fast), 地 precedes verbs (slowly 地 walks). Position is the key.

Pattern 12

比 (Comparisons)

Formula A + 比 + B + Adjective

比 (bǐ) means "compared to." The thing being compared goes first, followed by what it's compared to, then the adjective. No word for "more" needed - 比 already implies it.

Examples
  • 1
    他比我高|他比我高
    tā bǐ wǒ gāo
    "He is taller than me"
  • 2
    今天比昨天冷|今天比昨天冷
    jīntiān bǐ zuótiān lěng
    "Today is colder than yesterday"
  • 3
    中文比英文难|中文比英文難
    zhōngwén bǐ yīngwén nán
    "Chinese is harder than English"
  • 4
    坐高铁比开车快|坐高鐵比開車快
    zuò gāotiě bǐ kāichē kuài
    "Taking the HSR is faster than driving"
  • 5
    她比我大三岁|她比我大三歲
    tā bǐ wǒ dà sān suì
    "She is three years older than me"
Tip

Don't add 很 (very) after 比. Say 他比我高 not 他比我很高. For emphasis, use 多: 他比我高多了 ("He's much taller than me").

Pattern 13

Clause Connectors

Chinese uses paired connectors to link clauses. Unlike English where one conjunction is enough ("because X, Y"), Chinese typically uses both halves of the pair.

因為...所以 (yīnwèi...suǒyǐ)
"because...therefore" - cause and effect
  • 1
    因为下雨，所以我没出去|因為下雨，所以我沒出去
    yīnwèi xiàyǔ, suǒyǐ wǒ méi chūqù
    "Because it rained, I didn't go out"
  • 2
    因为太贵了，所以我没买|因為太貴了，所以我沒買
    yīnwèi tài guì le, suǒyǐ wǒ méi mǎi
    "Because it was too expensive, I didn't buy it"
虽然...但是 / 雖然...但是 (suīrán...dànshì)
"although...but" - concession
  • 1
    虽然很累，但是我很开心|雖然很累，但是我很開心
    suīrán hěn lèi, dànshì wǒ hěn kāixīn
    "Although I'm tired, I'm happy"
  • 2
    虽然他很忙，但是他还是来了|雖然他很忙，但是他還是來了
    suīrán tā hěn máng, dànshì tā háishì lái le
    "Although he was busy, he still came"
如果...就 (rúguǒ...jiù)
"if...then" - conditional
  • 1
    如果明天下雨，我就不去|如果明天下雨，我就不去
    rúguǒ míngtiān xiàyǔ, wǒ jiù bú qù
    "If it rains tomorrow, I won't go"
  • 2
    如果你喜欢，就买吧|如果你喜歡，就買吧
    rúguǒ nǐ xǐhuān, jiù mǎi ba
    "If you like it, then buy it"
Tip

In casual speech, one half can be dropped: 因为下雨我没出去 or 下雨所以我没出去. But using both halves is always correct and clearer.

Pattern 14

越来越 (More and More)

Formula 越来越 / 越來越 + Adjective/Verb

越来越 (yuè lái yuè) expresses something increasing over time - "more and more," "increasingly." It's one of the most useful patterns for describing change.

Examples
  • 1
    天气越来越冷了|天氣越來越冷了
    tiānqì yuè lái yuè lěng le
    "The weather is getting colder and colder"
  • 2
    你的中文越来越好了|你的中文越來越好了
    nǐ de zhōngwén yuè lái yuè hǎo le
    "Your Chinese is getting better and better"
  • 3
    生活越来越贵|生活越來越貴
    shēnghuó yuè lái yuè guì
    "Life is getting more and more expensive"
  • 4
    他越来越喜欢台湾了|他越來越喜歡台灣了
    tā yuè lái yuè xǐhuān Táiwān le
    "He likes Taiwan more and more"
  • 5
    我越来越忙了|我越來越忙了
    wǒ yuè lái yuè máng le
    "I'm getting busier and busier"
Tip

Related pattern: 越 A 越 B = "the more A, the more B." Example: 越吃越胖 (the more you eat, the fatter you get).

Pattern 15

一边...一边 (Doing Two Things at Once)

Formula 一边 + Verb A + 一边 + Verb B

一边...一边 (yì biān...yì biān) means "while doing X, also doing Y" - two actions happening simultaneously.

Examples
  • 1
    她一边吃饭一边看电视|她一邊吃飯一邊看電視
    tā yì biān chīfàn yì biān kàn diànshì
    "She eats while watching TV"
  • 2
    他一边走路一边听音乐|他一邊走路一邊聽音樂
    tā yì biān zǒulù yì biān tīng yīnyuè
    "He walks while listening to music"
  • 3
    我们一边喝茶一边聊天|我們一邊喝茶一邊聊天
    wǒmen yì biān hēchá yì biān liáotiān
    "We chat while drinking tea"
  • 4
    不要一边开车一边用手机|不要一邊開車一邊用手機
    bú yào yì biān kāichē yì biān yòng shǒujī
    "Don't use your phone while driving"
  • 5
    她一边唱歌一边跳舞|她一邊唱歌一邊跳舞
    tā yì biān chànggē yì biān tiàowǔ
    "She sings while dancing"
Tip

Both actions must be happening at the same time. You can't use 一边...一边 for sequential events. For "first X then Y," use 先...再 instead.

Pattern 16

Result Complements (Verb + Outcome)

Formula Verb + Result

Chinese puts the outcome immediately after the action. The verb says what you did; the complement says what happened because of it. English uses separate words ("finish eating"), but Chinese fuses them (吃完). Once you see the pattern, you can combine freely - you don't need to memorize every pair as a separate vocabulary word.

Examples
  • 1
    我看懂了|我看懂了
    wǒ kàn dǒng le
    "I understood it" (looked/read + understood)
  • 2
    我吃完饭了|我吃完飯了
    wǒ chī wán fàn le
    "I finished eating" (ate + finished)
  • 3
    作业做好了|作業做好了
    zuòyè zuò hǎo le
    "The homework is done" (did + completed properly)
  • 4
    你听懂了吗？|你聽懂了嗎？
    nǐ tīng dǒng le ma?
    "Did you understand?" (listened + understood)
  • 5
    他把车修好了|他把車修好了
    tā bǎ chē xiū hǎo le
    "He fixed the car" (repaired + good/done)

Common result elements - these snap onto many verbs:

Result Meaning Combinations
懂 understand 看懂, 听懂, 读懂
完 finish 吃完, 写完, 做完, 说完
好 done well / ready 做好, 准备好, 写好, 修好
到 reach / achieve 找到, 看到, 买到, 做到
Tip

Result complements pair naturally with 把 (Pattern 7): 我把功课做完了 - the 把 moves the object forward, and the complement says what happened to it. To negate a result complement, use 没 (not 不): 我没看懂, not 我不看懂.

Pattern 17

Directional Complements (Verb + Direction)

Formula Verb + Direction

Direction words snap onto verbs to show where the action goes. 来 means toward the speaker, 去 means away from the speaker. They combine with 进/出/上/下/过/回 for compound directions like 走进去 (walk + enter-away).

Examples
  • 1
    他走进去了|他走進去了
    tā zǒu jìnqù le
    "He walked inside" (walked + enter-away)
  • 2
    她跑出来了|她跑出來了
    tā pǎo chūlái le
    "She ran out" (ran + exit-toward)
  • 3
    把书拿出来|把書拿出來
    bǎ shū ná chūlái
    "Take the book out" (take + exit-toward)
  • 4
    他们搬上去了|他們搬上去了
    tāmen bān shàngqù le
    "They carried it upstairs" (moved + up-away)
  • 5
    快走过来！|快走過來！
    kuài zǒu guòlái!
    "Come over here quickly!" (walk + cross-toward)

Common directional pairs - 来 (toward speaker) vs 去 (away):

Direction Toward (来) Away (去)
in 进来|進來 进去|進去
out 出来|出來 出去|出去
up 上来|上來 上去|上去
down 下来|下來 下去|下去
over / across 过来|過來 过去|過去
back 回来|回來 回去|回去
Tip

When there's an object, it can go between the two parts of the directional complement: 走进教室去 (walk into the classroom). Simple directions (来/去) put the object before them: 拿书来 (bring the book).

Pattern 18

Location Words (位置词)

Formula Noun + Location Word

Location words come after the noun they describe - the opposite of English. English says "behind the restaurant"; Mandarin says "restaurant behind" (饭店后面). To say something is at a location, add 在 (Pattern 3) in front: 在 + Noun + Location Word - e.g. 猫在桌子下面 = "The cat is under the table."

Examples
  • 1
    银行旁边|銀行旁邊
    yínháng pángbiān
    "beside the bank" (bank + beside)
  • 2
    地铁站对面|地鐵站對面
    dìtiězhàn duìmiàn
    "across from the subway station"
  • 3
    饭店后面|飯店後面
    fàndiàn hòumiàn
    "behind the restaurant"
  • 4
    桌子上面|桌子上面
    zhuōzi shàngmiàn
    "on the table" (table + on top)
  • 5
    学校里面|學校裡面
    xuéxiào lǐmiàn
    "inside the school"

Pattern 19

The Sentence Grid (Position Changes Meaning)

Formula Subject + [Zone 1] + [Zone 2] + Verb + [Zone 3]

Chinese is SVO, but where a word lands relative to the verb changes its grammatical job completely. The same characters rearranged produce different meanings. Three zones surround every verb:

Zone 1: Auxiliary Zone 2: Pre-Verb Trait VERB Zone 3: Post-Verb Result
得 (děi) / 想 / 要 好 + Verb / 认真地 说 / 做 / 跑 得 + Assessment / 不 + Result
← What must be done? ← How do you enter the action? What was the outcome? →
Zone 1: Before Verb = Auxiliary / Obligation
Placing auxiliary operators before the main verb sets up necessity, possibility, or intention

When 得 appears before a verb, it is forced into the auxiliary role, triggering the pronunciation děi ("must / need to").

  • 1
    我得好好说|我得好好說
    wǒ děi hǎohāo shuō
    "I must speak properly" (得 = must, Zone 1)
  • 2
    你得早点走|你得早點走
    nǐ děi zǎo diǎn zǒu
    "You need to leave early" (得 = need to, Zone 1)
Zone 2: Before Verb = Trait, Attitude, or Entry Condition
Anything placed before the verb modifies the state of the subject going into the action

好 + Verb creates a compound adjective meaning "easy to [Verb]" or "pleasant to [Verb]." Adverbs like 认真地 (seriously) and 慢慢 (slowly) also live in Zone 2 - they describe how you enter the action.

  • 1
    我好说|我好說
    wǒ hǎo shuō
    "I am easy to talk to" (好 = easy to, Zone 2)

Other examples of 好 + Verb:

好吃 Easy/pleasant to eat → Delicious
好看 Easy/pleasant to look at → Pretty / Good-looking
好用 Easy to use → User-friendly
好听 | 好聽 Easy/pleasant to listen to → Nice-sounding
Zone 3: After Verb = Result, Assessment, or Target
When something goes after the verb, it tells the listener what happened or how well it was done

Pattern A: Direct Resultative Complement (Verb + Result)

Placing an adjective directly after the verb without 得 turns it into a result. It answers: "What was the final settled state?"

  • 1
    我说好了|我說好了
    wǒ shuō hǎo le
    "I agreed / settled it" (说 + 好 = spoke until settled)
看见 | 看見 Look + Perceive → To see
做完 Do + Finished → To finish doing
听懂 | 聽懂 Listen + Understand → To understand what you heard

Pattern B: Degree / Manner Complement (Verb + 得 + Assessment)

To evaluate the quality, speed, or manner of the action rather than its completed result, use 得 as an assessment bridge.

  • 1
    我说得好|我說得好
    wǒ shuō de hǎo
    "I speak well" (说 + 得 + 好 = speak + evaluation + high quality)

Pattern C: Potential Complement (Verb + 得/不 + Result)

Insert 得 between verb and result for "able to achieve result." Replace 得 with 不 for "unable to achieve result."

Positive (Can) Negative (Cannot) Meaning
做得到 zuò de dào 做不到 zuò bù dào Can / cannot achieve it
听得懂 | 聽得懂 tīng de dǒng 听不懂 | 聽不懂 tīng bù dǒng Can / cannot understand by listening
吃得完 chī de wán 吃不完 chī bù wán Can / cannot finish eating
看得见 | 看得見 kàn de jiàn 看不见 | 看不見 kàn bù jiàn Can / cannot see
The 说/好 Cheat Sheet - Same Words, Four Meanings
Zone Construction Logic Example
Zone 1 得 (děi) + Verb Obligation: "What must be done?" 我得说 - I must speak
Zone 2 好 + Verb Trait: "Is this easy to do?" 我好说 - I'm easy to talk to
Zone 3A Verb + 好 Result: "What was the settled state?" 我说好 - I agreed
Zone 3B Verb + 得 + 好 Assessment: "How well was it done?" 我说得好 - I speak well
Tip

This grid connects several patterns you already know: modals like 想/要 (Pattern 6) are Zone 1, 地 adverbs (Pattern 11) are Zone 2, 得 complements (Pattern 10) and result complements (Pattern 16) are Zone 3. The grid is the map - those patterns are the close-ups.

Pattern 20

Pre-Verb Adverbs (还, 也, 都, 再, 又, 就, 才)

Formula Subject + Time + Adverb + Verb + Object

These seven adverbs all sit in the same slot: after the subject, before the verb. They never go after the verb. English often handles these with word order or stress; Chinese uses a dedicated word each time.

Where Adverbs Sit in S + T + V + O (Pattern 1)
Subject Time Adverb Verb Object
我 昨天 也 吃了 饺子 | 餃子
她 每天 都 喝 茶
他 明天 还 | 還 要去 机场 | 機場

S + T + Adverb + V + O - the adverb never moves after the verb.

还 (hái) - Still / Also / Even More
Continuation, addition, or intensified comparison
  • 1
    他还在睡觉|他還在睡覺
    tā hái zài shuìjiào
    "He is still sleeping" (continuation)
  • 2
    我还想喝咖啡|我還想喝咖啡
    wǒ hái xiǎng hē kāfēi
    "I also want coffee" (addition)
也 (yě) - Also / Too
Echoes what someone else said or did - "me too," "same here"
  • 3
    我也喜欢|我也喜歡
    wǒ yě xǐhuan
    "I like it too"
  • 4
    她也是老师|她也是老師
    tā yě shì lǎoshī
    "She is also a teacher"
都 (dōu) - All / Both
Sums up everyone or everything before it - the group acts as one
  • 5
    我们都去|我們都去
    wǒmen dōu qù
    "We all go"
  • 6
    什么都可以|什麼都可以
    shénme dōu kěyǐ
    "Anything is fine" (everything + all = anything)
再 (zài) vs 又 (yòu) - Again
Both mean "again," but 再 looks forward (will do again) and 又 looks back (did again)
  • 7
    请再说一次|請再說一次
    qǐng zài shuō yī cì
    "Please say it again" (future - hasn't happened yet)
  • 8
    他又迟到了|他又遲到了
    tā yòu chídào le
    "He was late again" (past - already happened, implies annoyance)
就 (jiù) vs 才 (cái) - Then / Only Then
Both connect a condition to what follows, but 就 = "as soon as / right away" and 才 = "not until / only then"
  • 9
    他八点就来了|他八點就來了
    tā bā diǎn jiù lái le
    "He came as early as 8" (sooner than expected)
  • 10
    他十点才来|他十點才來
    tā shí diǎn cái lái
    "He didn't come until 10" (later than expected)
Quick Reference
Adverb Core Meaning Example
还 | 還 hái still / also / even more 还在睡 - still sleeping
也 yě also / too (matching) 我也要 - I want some too
都 dōu all / both (totality) 都知道 - everyone knows
再 zài again (future) 再试 - try again
又 yòu again (past) 又忘了 - forgot again
就 jiù right away / as early as 马上就走 - leaving right now
才 cái not until / only then 刚才到 - just now arrived
Easily Confused Pairs

还 vs 也: 也 echoes ("you like it? me too"). 还 adds ("I want tea, and also coffee").

再 vs 又: 再 = will happen again (再见 "see you again"). 又 = happened again (又下雨了 "it rained again").

就 vs 才: Same event, opposite judgment. 他八点就来了 = he came at 8 (early!). 他八点才来 = he didn't come until 8 (late!).

Tip

还 (hái) the adverb is a different word from 还 (huán) "to return / give back." Same character, different tone. 还钱 | 還錢 (huán qián) = return money; 还有钱 | 還有錢 (hái yǒu qián) = still have money.
