Pinyin IME Guide

Why Can't I Find My Character?

You heard a sound, typed your best guess, and the IME doesn't show your character.
This guide helps you figure out what to try next.

Quick Swap Cheat Sheet

When the IME doesn't show your character, try these swaps in order. Most problems are solved by the first two.

Initials (consonant starts)

You typed Try instead Why it happens
b- p- Add air (soft vs puffy)
d- t- Add air (soft vs puffy)
g- k- Add air (soft vs puffy)
zh- z- or j- Curled vs flat vs squeaky tongue
ch- c- or q- Curled vs flat vs squeaky tongue
sh- s- or x- Curled vs flat vs squeaky tongue
j- zh- or z- Squeaky vs curled vs flat tongue
q- ch- or c- Squeaky vs curled vs flat tongue
x- sh- or s- Squeaky vs curled vs flat tongue
Vowel is i or ü? Skip the curled (zh/ch/sh) and flat (z/c/s) rows - go straight to j/q/x. That eliminates 6 of 9 options.

Finals (vowel endings)

You typed Try instead Why it happens
-an -ang Missed the -g (most common IME miss)
-ang -an Added an extra -g
-en -eng Same -n/-ng swap
-in -ing Same -n/-ng swap
-ui -iu "way" vs "yo" - swapped order
-iu -ui Same, other direction
-ei -ie "ay" vs "yeh"
-ou -uo "oh" vs "waw"
-e -o "uh" vs "aw" - sound similar
-u -v (ü) Forgot the dots - see ü key below
Don't memorize the table. Memorize 3 rules:
• N vs NG — "Did I forget the G?" (an↔ang, en↔eng, in↔ing)
• Reversed vowels — "Which vowel comes first?" (ui↔iu, ei↔ie, ou↔uo)
• E vs O and U vs Ü — the leftover pair swaps

The whole system in one line: N/NG → Reverse → E/O → Ü
If your character doesn't appear, ask in this order:
1. Did I confuse N and NG? (an↔ang)
2. Did I reverse two vowels? (ui↔iu, ei↔ie, ou↔uo)
3. Did I confuse E and O? (e↔o)
4. Did I mean ü? (u↔v, especially nü/lü)

Sound Families

Mnemonics for the three confusing rows. Each family has the same airflow pattern: soft → puffy → hissy. Remember the group names: "JaQX" (sounds like "jacks") and "ZoCS" (sounds like "socks").

The "JaQX" (jacks) - Squeaky Clean Clique (j, q, x)

These are the high-pitched snobs. They ONLY hang out with i and ü.

j = the Jeep · q = the bird that goes "Cheep" (j + air) · x = the Sheep

Jeep won't pick you up? Try the Cheeping bird (q). Bird flies away? Find the Sheep (x). If it sounds like a squeaky toy, it's this club.

The "ZoCS" (socks) - Flat & Buzzy Snakes (z, c, s)

Tongue stays flat behind your teeth. No curling, no H.

z = suDS (buzz, no air) · c = piTS (spitting air) · s = Snake (sssss)

Zebra (z) too quiet? Try the spitting Cat (c). Cat runs off? Follow the Snake (s). If you add an H, the snake gets angry.

The "Curled-Up Cavemen" (zh, ch, sh)

Tongue curled back. The H-versions of the snakes - deeper and thicker.

zh = the Judge (deep "jr") · ch = the Chimp in church (screaming air) · sh = She is shushing you

Judge (zh) ignores you? Ask the Chimp (ch). Chimp too loud? She (sh) will shush you. Curl your tongue like you're trying to swallow it.

The 3-family shortcut: If the vowel after it is i or ü, skip the snakes and cavemen - go straight to the Squeaky Clean clique (j/q/x). That eliminates 6 of the 9 options instantly.

The ü Key

The ü sound (say "ee" with rounded lips) is typed with the V key on most IMEs.

You type You get Notes
nv nü 女 nü (woman) - must use V
lv lü 绿 lü (green) - must use V
ju jü After j, q, x the ü is hidden - just type U
qu qü 去 qü (go) - just type QU, not QV
xu xü 需 xü (need) - just type XU, not XV
yuan üan 远 yuán (far) - Y is the bodyguard for ü
When to use V: Only after n and l. After j, q, x the ü is disguised as U (the IME knows). After y just type YU.

The Bodyguard Rule (W and Y)

Syllables that start with a vowel sound get a "bodyguard" consonant in pinyin spelling. This is what you actually type in the IME.

You hear You type Example
u- sound w- hear "oo" → type wu (五 five)
ua, uo, uai ... wa, wo, wai ... hear "waw" → type wo (我 I/me)
i- sound y- hear "ee" → type yi (一 one)
ia, ie, iao ... ya, ye, yao ... hear "yow" → type yao (要 want)
ü- sound yu- hear "ü" → type yu (鱼 fish)
üe, üan, ün yue, yuan, yun hear "you-eh" → type yue (月 moon)
If a syllable sounds like it starts with a pure vowel, it never does in pinyin. Add Y for i/ü sounds or W for u sounds.
