3 Rules for Using Adjectives

Rule 1
The "Very" Tax - 很 (hěn)

To say "The coffee is hot," you can't just say 咖啡热. A bare adjective in a sentence sounds like a comparison ("Coffee is hot [but tea isn't]"). You need 很 as filler to make it a neutral statement. It technically means "very" but here it's just grammatical glue.

咖啡很热 (咖啡很熱) kāfēi hěn rè "The coffee is hot" (neutral statement)
他很高 tā hěn gāo "He is tall"
Rule 2
The "Noun Glue" - 的 (de)

To put an adjective before a noun (like "a big dog"), use 的 between them. For single-syllable adjectives like 大, you can sometimes skip 的 (大狗), but as a beginner, using it is always safe.

大的狗 dà de gǒu "a big dog"
便宜的东西 (便宜的東西) piányi de dōngxi "cheap stuff"
Rule 3
The "Not" - 不 (bù)

To negate an adjective, put 不 in front. No 很 needed here - 不 replaces it.

我不忙 wǒ bù máng "I'm not busy"
这个不贵 (這個不貴) zhège bù guì "This isn't expensive"
大 小 好 坏 多 少
