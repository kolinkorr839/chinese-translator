Initials (Consonants)

Pinyin Sounds like Tips
b"b" in "bed"Unaspirated (less breathy than English "b")
p"p" in "put"Aspirated (a puff of air)
m"m" in "mom"Same as English
f"f" in "fan"Same as English
d"d" in "day"Unaspirated
t"t" in "top"Aspirated
n"n" in "now"Same as English
l"l" in "learn"Same as English
g"g" in "go"Unaspirated
k"k" in "kite"Aspirated
h"h" in "help"Slightly stronger, from back of throat
j"j" in "jeep"Tongue flat, pressed against roof of mouth
q"ch" in "cheese"Like "j" but with a puff of air
x"sh" in "she"Tongue flat behind lower teeth (not curled back like sh)
z"dz" in "kids"Tongue flat behind lower teeth
c"ts" in "cats"Tongue flat with a puff of air
s"s" in "see"Tongue flat behind lower teeth
zh"j" in "jump"Tongue curled back (retroflex). Like "dr" said quickly.
ch"ch" in "church"Tongue curled back with a puff of air
sh"sh" in "she"Tongue curled back
rBetween "r" and "zh"Like "pleasure" but with tongue curled back
y"y" in "yes"Same as English
w"w" in "want"Same as English
The hardest distinction for English speakers: zh/ch/sh (tongue curled back) vs z/c/s (tongue flat) vs j/q/x (tongue behind lower teeth). Don't stress about perfecting these right away - you will be understood even if they are not perfect.

After j, q, x, the letter "u" is always pronounced "ü" (the dots are dropped in writing but the sound stays). So "qu" is pronounced "chü", not "koo".
The letter i after zh, ch, sh, r, z, c, s is NOT pronounced "ee". It is a buzzy, held consonant sound - almost silent. Just hold the consonant. This is one of the few sounds you simply need to hear and imitate.
